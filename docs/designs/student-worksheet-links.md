# Design: Student worksheet links with submitted answers

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-09-03
Branch: main
Repo: andrew-wen2/tutor-app
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Startup

**Implementation detail lives in the approved plan, not here.** This document is the
*why*: the diagnosis, the demand evidence, the premises, and the approaches that were
rejected. Do not duplicate the schema or route specs into this file — they will drift.

## Problem Statement

SessionDesk generates difficulty-calibrated problems and none of them reach students
between sessions. Homework comes from a textbook. The tutor assigns work and has no way
to see whether it was done.

The ask started as "a student-facing side where I can assign readings and problems and
see their progress." The diagnostic narrowed it to something smaller and sharper: a
delivery path with a **return path**, so completion becomes observable.

## Demand Evidence

Weak on the student side, strong on the tutor side. Stated plainly because it sets the
risk profile:

- **No student or parent has asked for this.** The pain is the tutor's — "I assign but
  can't see completion."
- **Zero students have used any version of this.** Nobody has opened a link, submitted
  an answer, or seen a problem outside a session.
- **The tutor-side pain is real and specific:** 15+ paying students, homework assigned
  from a textbook, no completion signal.

The riskiest assumption is that a student will open a link and type answers into it. It
is untested, cannot be tested by writing code, and is the first gate in the plan.

## Status Quo

**Outbound delivery already exists and goes unused.** `components/SessionDetail.tsx:653`
renders a `DownloadGroup` (PDF + DOCX). `lib/download-problems.ts:103` splits the export
correctly: *"a student worksheet (questions only) followed by a tutor answer key."*
A worksheet can be handed to a student today.

It isn't, because **nothing comes back from a .docx.** The textbook wins on the return
path: it has an answer key the student can check themselves. Generated problems have
neither self-check nor reporting.

The blocker is the inbound half, not the outbound half. This corrected the initial
framing ("no way to deliver it") and shrank the build considerably.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target: contest students** — gated on `calibrationFor({ profile }).competition` being
non-null. (There is no per-student plan object; `GenerationPlan` is derived per request,
and `competition` is set only on the corpus fast path.)

Two reasons, aligned:
1. The pipeline's best path only fires for them — corpus anchoring, difficulty bands, and
   the seed/variant adapt route all gate on a competition being present. Everyone else
   runs the model path with zero anchors.
2. They are the profile that does optional math voluntarily. A scoreboard exists.

**Wedge:** one problem set per booked session, delivered as a link, answers typed back,
graded server-side, results visible to the tutor before the next session.

**Out of v1:** readings (content storage — the book/PDF library was built and
deliberately removed), non-contest subjects, student accounts, multiple sets per session,
retries, spaced repetition, instant per-problem feedback, a calendar-level "needs a set"
indicator.

## This overrides a stated non-goal

CLAUDE.md Non-goals lists "Parent portal, roles/permissions, sharing." **This design is
sharing, and the student is a second principal** — authenticated by bearer token rather
than by cookie, but a principal. The app goes from one authorization model to two.

Calling that "staying inside the non-goals" was rationalization in the first draft.
CLAUDE.md's own precedent is that non-goals get overridden by *explicit request* — as the
`ReferenceProblem` corpus, multi-user auth, and Sign in with Google each were — not by
argument. Plan approval was that override.

What the token approach *does* buy, honestly: no login UI, no password reset, no session
management, no account recovery for a minor, and an accounts migration that is purely
additive. Real surface reduction. Not zero.

## Premises

1. **The customer is the tutor.** Success = the tutor gains completion visibility.
   Student satisfaction is the means.
2. **The return path is the blocker.** Outbound delivery already ships.
3. **Contest students only in v1.**
4. **No student accounts in v1** — bearer-token links, with the non-goal override above
   stated rather than argued away. Accounts are explicitly wanted later.
5. **Generation and Send are separate clicks.** *(Revised twice. The original — "session
   creation fires generation" — is infeasible; see below. The tutor generates with the
   existing button, reviews what's on screen, then sends. The review between the two
   clicks is the quality gate.)*
6. **AoPS Alcumus substitutes for the content layer only.** It is contest-grade, free,
   and adaptive — and gives the tutor nothing: no assignment, no visibility, no
   continuity with what was covered Tuesday. DeltaMath is not a comparison; it does not
   do competition math. Every practice platform in this space builds for the student as
   buyer. In 1:1 tutoring the tutor is the buyer and the one flying blind.

### Why generation cannot fire on booking

Three independent grounds, all verified:

1. **Budget.** `app/api/sessions/route.ts` sets `maxDuration = 60`; `/api/generate` sets
   `300`. Work in `after()` bills to the creating invocation. Hard-tier generation does
   not fit in 60s, and raising the create route to 300s makes every booking hold a
   five-minute function.
2. **Series multiplier.** `parseOccurrences` allows up to 52 occurrences. A weekly
   recurring booking would fire up to 52 sequential Opus generations in one `after()` —
   a multi-dollar accident per click that times out long before occurrence five.
3. **Empty topic.** The create route stamps `topic: ""` on every occurrence after the
   first (commit `cbca857`); topic autosaves later on SessionDetail. A set generated at
   create time is calibrated on nothing.

(The existing `after()` block in that route is also gated on `if (account)` — it only runs
when Google Calendar is connected. It was never a general-purpose hook.)

## Approaches Considered

**A: Worksheet link + submissions — CHOSEN.** Token on `Session`, public `/w/[token]`,
`Submission` keyed to `studentId`, results on `SessionDetail`.

**B: `Assignment` as a first-class model.** Rejected as speculative schema — zero
submissions exist, and CLAUDE.md already made this call for payments ("no Payment table
until partial payments exist — YAGNI"). Revisit when a second set per session is actually
wanted.

**C: Self-grading offline worksheet.** Extend the HTML export to grade in-browser;
completion returns as a code the student texts back. Rejected: the return path is manual,
which contradicts premise 1 and is exactly the step that stops happening by week three.

## The load-bearing decision

**The token is not the identity.** `Submission` carries `studentId`; the token is a
bearer credential that *resolves* to a `studentId`. The tutor mints it and already knows
whose it is.

This costs one column today and makes the stated accounts migration purely additive:
accounts become "a second way to authenticate as that same `studentId`," with every
historical submission already correctly attributed. Keying submissions to the token
instead would mean a backfill against real student history, guessing token → person.

"Past sessions for a student" is then `Session.findMany({ where: { studentId } })` — the
query the tutor-side student page already runs, with a different principal.

## Known repo gap this surfaced

`lib/generation/answer-match.ts:7` promises loose mode accepts `x=2` for a stored `2`.
It does not: `normalizeString("x=2")` → `"x=2"`, `stripTrailingWords` requires a leading
digit so it no-ops, `tryParseNumber` returns null, and contest answers are `integer` so
the sorted-token fallback never fires. It returns `false`. The only loose behaviour that
exists is `"42 apples"` → `"42"`, covered by one test.

This is a live gap independent of this feature. The fix is step 8 of the plan.

## Success Criteria

1. **A student submits at least one set.** Binary. Nothing else matters until true.
2. **≥ half the contest roster submits at least once within three weeks of first send.**
   Below that, delivery was not the blocker and the diagnosis was wrong.
3. **The tutor opens a results view before a session** — the visibility from premise 1,
   actually used.
4. **Wrong answers change the next session's `topic`.** The loop closing back into the
   learning history is the thing no other platform can do.

Explicitly not success criteria: sets generated, links sent, page views.

## Gates before building

1. **The zero-code demand test.** Hand your most contest-focused student the DOCX
   worksheet that already exports today and ask them to bring back answers. If they do —
   even partial, even late — build. If they don't, ask once why, and don't build until
   the answer is one you believe.
2. **Measure per-set generation cost** via `lib/generation/pricing.ts` on one real
   hard-tier set. If it is dollars per set, the wedge economics change and this should be
   re-decided, not discovered later.

## Review history

Two rounds of adversarial review against the repo. Round 1: 6/10, 15 issues. Round 2:
7/10, 23 issues. Three findings were load-bearing and reversed decisions made during the
session:

- **Loose-mode grading was claimed as free reuse and is not** (above).
- **Auto-generation on booking was infeasible** on three independent grounds; premise 5
  was withdrawn and replaced.
- **"No second principal" was a semantic dodge**, and `AppShell`'s `BARE_PATHS` is
  exact-match — so as originally specced, an unauthenticated student would have seen the
  full tutor sidebar including Sign out.

## What I noticed about how you think

- You said **"DeltaMath doesn't do competition math."** Five words, and it killed my
  comparison outright. I'd anchored on the wrong incumbent and you corrected it from
  domain knowledge, not from argument. That correction surfaced the actual insight —
  Alcumus and DeltaMath both build for the student as buyer, and nobody builds for the
  tutor.

- You rejected the same question three times rather than picking a nearest-fit answer.
  Most people take the closest option to move on. Each rejection was hiding a real
  constraint — the generation trigger, the account sequencing, the past-sessions view —
  and none would have surfaced if you'd just clicked B.

- You asked for **"a student-facing side"** and the pain you actually named was **"I
  assign but can't see completion."** Those are different products. You reached for a
  solution shape before the problem was pinned, which is normal — what wasn't normal is
  that you didn't defend the original framing once it moved.

- Your own comment in `answer-match.ts` says *"the deferred worksheet: did the student's
  typed answer match the stored one?"* You designed this feature's hardest primitive
  before you asked for the feature, wrote the comment describing what it should do, and
  then didn't finish it. Worth noticing what else you've already half-built and forgotten.
